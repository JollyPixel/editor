# Every editor, 26 November 2026

What it costs to open each of the nine editors the studio serves, what they cost once open, how long the shell takes to boot, what typing costs in the script editor, two experiments on the largest costs found, and the fixes built from them. Measured at `ef13b5bf3` on a built studio served by [`perf/serve/build.ts`](../../perf/serve/build.ts). The method and the tools are in [Benchmarking editor opens](./benchmarking.md).

The machine ran in its fast state: a fresh browser's first `requestAdapter` took 141 ms before the session and 198 ms after it. Tables give medians in milliseconds unless they say otherwise.

## Summary

- Two fixes came out of this run (see [Fixes](#fixes)): editors render uncapped, and the preview frame waits for editors to boot. Against the build before them, warm opens got 24 to 163 ms faster and cold opens 61 to 106 ms faster across scene, environment, model, voxel-map and voxel-model, and scene and model orbit at 60 renders/s instead of 31.
- Scene, model and environment render at 31 frames per second while the camera moves, on a 60 Hz display. `configureRuntimeDevice` caps the loop at the frame rate `@pmndrs/detect-gpu` predicts for the GPU, and only voxel-map and voxel-model pass `maxFps: Infinity`. With the benchmark skipped, the same editors render at 60.
- The same benchmark downloads a JSON table from `unpkg.com` and creates a WebGL context on every runtime start. Skipping it makes a scene open 99 to 133 ms faster cold and 41 ms faster warm, and an environment open 103 to 161 ms faster cold.
- The first click of the double-click selects the row, and the Asset Browser then asks the hidden preview frame for a thumbnail. For a voxel model that boots a second WebGPU runtime next to the editor. Without previews, a voxel-model open is 107 to 109 ms faster warm and cold, and a voxel-map open 23 to 91 ms faster.
- The largest remaining cost of a 3D open is the first-frame pipeline compile: 22 to 26 shader modules through DXC, 170 to 240 ms of `host.boot:compile` on a warm scene or voxel open. A browser restart with warm disk caches is no faster than a reload, so the compile is paid on every open.
- Editors without a 3D runtime open in 60 to 140 ms warm: script, texture, pixel-art and sound. Sound reports `ready` at 140 ms and settles 420 to 570 ms later, once the off-thread decode of the 190 s seed track ends.
- In the script editor, TypeScript, JavaScript and JSON files of 100 KB answer Enter in 35 to 45 ms, and any 100 KB file takes a 4 KB paste in 68 to 72 ms. Scrolling a 100 KB TypeScript file has a median latency of 48 ms, with 13 of 60 wheel steps over 50 ms.

## Measurement setup

The harness gained two targets for this run. `environment` creates `New environment.environment.json` from the New asset menu. `texture` drops `packages/voxel-renderer/docs/images/shapes.jpg` (63 KB) on the Asset Browser: a dropped PNG becomes a pixel-art asset through its conversion, so the texture viewer needs another image format. The terrain editor has no target: the studio project template registers neither `@jolly-pixel/editor.terrain` nor the terrain kind.

The model target now drops `ToyCar.glb` from `packages/backend.three/examples`, where the engine split moved it. Offline, the draft row of a new asset and the created row briefly coexist, so the harness now waits for the first matching row. The profile reader resolves the chunks the studio serves from `/assets/` through the studio's source maps; until now it attributed them all to `(studio shell)`.

The sound seed is the local 190 s track of earlier reports (1.7 MB Ogg). Every other seed is in the repository.

### Where a voxel open goes

Boot steps of a warm reload online, start → end from the double-click:

| Step | voxel-map | voxel-model |
|---|---|---|
| `editor.runtime:renderer` | 79 → 141 | 95 → 186 |
| `host.boot:session` | 121 → 160 | 160 → 217 |
| `editor.runtime:first frame` | 167 → 196 | 224 → 248 |
| `host.boot:mount` | 160 → 219 | 217 → 277 |
| `host.boot:compile` | 246 → 487 | 286 → 482 |

A Chrome trace of each warm open shows the main thread mostly idle during `compile`. The GPU process compiles 22 (voxel-map), 26 (voxel-model) and 26 (model) shader modules through DXC, 237 to 563 ms on worker threads, and the page waits on `CommandBufferProxyImpl::WaitForGetOffset` for 35 to 89 ms. The first frame of voxel-map is one 102 ms animation frame.

In the CPU profile of a warm voxel-map open, the 615 ms after the click split into 206 ms of `(program)`, 125 ms of native calls, 84 ms of three.js, 30 ms of Lit, 19 ms of voxel-renderer and 14 ms of pixel-draw-renderer. Nothing in the editors' own code reached 5 ms. `editor:metrics`, which overlaps `compile` in the step table, costs 1 to 3 ms of CPU.

### Model and sound after `ready`

Model reports `ready` before its first render of the glTF scene. On first, cold and reload opens, the 214 to 302 ms of main-thread work after `ready` is that render building node materials (75 ms under three.js `update`, 32 ms in TSL `build`) and parsing the glTF. Those opens settle 250 to 400 ms after `ready`; a reopen settles 160 to 170 ms after.

Sound's `ready` stopped waiting for the decode in November. The main thread is idle for most of the 420 to 570 ms between `ready` and `settled`: Chrome decodes the 190 s track off-thread, then `WaveformPeaks` scans it in 26 ms. The 1.6 s dev seed would settle within a few frames.

## The GPU benchmark

`configureRuntimeDevice` in `@jolly-pixel/runtime` sets `loop.scheduler.maxFps` to the `fps` that `getGPUTier()` predicts, unless the caller passes `maxFps`. `getGPUTier()` creates a WebGL context to read the renderer string, then fetches `https://unpkg.com/@pmndrs/detect-gpu@6.0.30/dist/benchmarks/d-intel.json` for this laptop. Voxel-map and voxel-model pass `maxFps: Infinity`; scene, model and environment do not, and neither does the hidden preview frame.

The variant `no-gpu-tier` skips the benchmark and leaves `maxFps` at `Infinity`. Four interleaved rounds online ([plan](./full-run-2026-11-26-gpu-tier.json)), `ready` against the control:

| Editor | first | cold | reload | restart |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scene | 601 → 468 (−133) | 547 → 448 (−99) | 375 → 334 (−41) | 389 → 375 (−14) |
| environment | 426 → 265 (−161) | 342 → 239 (−103) | 175 → 162 (−13) | 265 → 234 (−31) |
| voxel-model | 752 → 746 (−6) | 651 → 649 (−2) | 538 → 518 (−20) | 592 → 585 (−7) |

Voxel-model `settled` moves by −7 to −174 ms instead. Its profile still shows `benchmarkFps`, most likely from the preview frame. The `script ms` column of the variant rises by about 100 ms because the editors render at 60 frames per second during the 4 s settle window.

Steady state, two rounds of 5 s each, control before and after the variant:

| Editor | Scenario | renders/s control | renders/s `no-gpu-tier` | main thread ms/s control | main thread ms/s `no-gpu-tier` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| scene | orbit | 31 | 60 | 141 to 144 | 156 |
| model | orbit | 31 | 60 | 139 to 142 | 134 |
| scene | hover | 9.3 | 18 | 56 | 72 |
| model | hover | 9.3 | 18 | 56 to 58 | 56 |

Doubling the render rate cost scene 12 to 15 ms/s of main thread and model nothing measurable. The 3 November report's steady table already showed scene at 31 renders/s.

From the code and not measured: the same `configureRuntimeDevice` runs in an exported game, so a game also asks unpkg.com on start and caps itself at the predicted rate.

## The selection preview

The Asset Browser renders `asset-details` for the selected row with a thumbnail from `AssetPreviewCache`. Kinds with a 3D preview are rendered by a hidden 1 px preview frame that loads its own runtime; a texture's preview is the image itself, read over the socket. The double-click's first click selects the row, so every open from the tree also starts a preview.

The variant `no-preview` hands `asset-details` no preview cache. Three interleaved rounds online ([plan](./full-run-2026-11-26-preview.json)), `ready` against the control:

| Editor | first | cold | reload |
|---|---|---|---|
| voxel-model | 699 → 695 (−4) | 675 → 566 (−109) | 548 → 441 (−107) |
| voxel-map | 810 → 721 (−89) | 665 → 574 (−91) | 487 → 464 (−23) |
| texture | 151 → 152 (+1) | 147 → 155 (+8) | 86 → 90 (+4) |

Without the preview a texture open receives 63 KB over the socket instead of 125 KB, and a voxel-map open 12 KB instead of 26 KB. On a cold open the preview frame also downloads about 420 KB of shared chunks while the editor loads; with the preview off, the editor frame downloads them itself (229 → 653 KB in its own resource timing).

## Fixes

Two changes were built after the experiments:

- `EditorRuntime.load` passes `maxFps: Infinity` when neither `?max-fps=` nor the editor sets one, so no editor runs the GPU benchmark or contacts unpkg.com, and voxel-map and voxel-model no longer pass it themselves. Games are unchanged: they still benchmark, fetch the table and cap their frame rate.
- `PreviewFrame` holds a render for 400 ms after the request, then until no editor tab is `booting` or `switching` (`EditorTabs.untilIdle()`), for at most 5 s. A cached thumbnail and a texture preview, which the server answers, show at once; an uncached 3D thumbnail of a plain selection now shows 400 ms later.

Confirmed with three interleaved rounds online against the build without them, served side by side ([plan](./full-run-2026-11-26-fixes.json)), `ready` against the control:

| Editor | first | cold | reload |
|---|---|---|---|
| scene | 619 → 475 (−144) | 548 → 450 (−98) | 382 → 321 (−61) |
| environment | 439 → 262 (−177) | 344 → 238 (−106) | 174 → 150 (−24) |
| model | 447 → 316 (−131) | 410 → 306 (−104) | 334 → 171 (−163) |
| voxel-map | 813 → 742 (−71) | 695 → 599 (−96) | 583 → 444 (−139) |
| voxel-model | 799 → 697 (−102) | 641 → 580 (−61) | 510 → 402 (−108) |

`settled` follows `ready` everywhere except one cell: model `cold` settles at 883 ms against 703, with the same main-thread work after `ready` (214 against 206 ms). It was not investigated. Steady state, orbiting: scene and model render at 60/s instead of 31, for 5 to 26 ms/s more main thread.

## Recommendations

In order of measured gain for the effort:

1. Decide whether games keep the GPU-tier cap and the unpkg.com request. `getGPUTier({ benchmarksURL })` can serve the benchmark files from the game's own origin, which keeps the cap without the third-party request.
2. Let the texture preview reuse the bytes the editor already holds instead of reading the image twice (63 KB per open here).
3. Reduce what the first frame compiles. At 170 to 240 ms per warm 3D open, the pipeline compile is now the largest single step, and disk caches do not help it. Counting the distinct pipelines each editor builds at open, and which of them the first frame needs, is the next measurement.
4. Report model `ready` after its first glTF render, or compile its materials before `ready`, so the tab spinner covers the 300 to 400 ms the model editor keeps the main thread busy.
5. Profile scrolling and pasting in 100 KB files. Layout and style dominate both, not script.

Not measured, as leads: voxel-map's idle 43 ms/s, voxel-model's major GC steps while hovering, the terrain editor (not registered in the studio), and every open on a throttled link.

## Follow-up

Recommendations 1, 3, 4 and 5 were worked through the same evening; 2 waits for a throttled run to show a cost.

- Games (1): `load()` without `maxFps` no longer runs `getGPUTier()`. It renders at the display rate, and a `FrameRateGovernor` halves the rate while a quarter of the frames in 2 s miss their refresh. `@pmndrs/detect-gpu` is gone from `@jolly-pixel/runtime`.
- Model (4): `ready` waits for the first read of the model file, so `boot:compile` now compiles the glTF materials.
- Compile (3): `pipelineCensus` counted what each warm open builds (table below), and found that the environment editor's `boot:compile` ended before its first canvas frame. `compiledFrame()` now waits two frames before it looks at pending compiles, because a scene that `load()` just activated awakes on the first and draws on the second.
- Scripts (5): traced with `perf/edit/measure.ts --trace`.

### Pipelines of a warm open

`perf/probes/pipelineCensus.ts`, offline, one warm open each, times in ms after the double-click:

| Editor | pipelines | shader modules | first pipeline created | compile | drawn by its end | created, not drawn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| voxel-map | 11 | 21 | 257 | 232 → 457 | 9 | 2 (`MeshLambertMaterial`) |
| voxel-model | 12 | 19 | 241 | 227 → 421 | 9 | 3 (`ShadowMaterial`, `MeshBasicMaterial` ×2) |
| scene | 9 | 14 | 186 | 173 → 341 | 9 | 0 |
| model | 14 | 20 | 166 | 322 → 482 | 14 | 0 |
| environment, before the fix | 5 | 9 | 158 | 134 → 143 | 0 | 0 |
| environment, after | 5 | 9 | 158 | 138 → 273 | 5 | 0 |

Every open draws almost every pipeline it builds, so compiling less has little room: two or three pipelines in the voxel editors. The renderer is ready at 90 to 143 ms, but the first pipeline is only created at 158 to 257 ms, after `session` and `mount`, and the pipelines of one wave become ready together 90 to 150 ms after they were created. Starting the compile of the pipelines every editor draws (`outputColorTransform`, background, sprites, basic and line materials) while `session` and `mount` run is the remaining lead, worth up to that gap: 48 ms in scene, 140 ms in voxel-map. Voxel-map's two `MeshLambertMaterial` pipelines start a second wave at 385 ms and end `compile` about 50 ms after the first wave.

Opens after the changes, three runs online, `ready` (`settled` in parentheses where it differs):

| Editor | cold | reload |
|---|---|---|
| scene | 464 | 324 |
| voxel-map | 604 | 418 |
| environment | 412 | 304 |
| model | 598 (877) | 420 (714) |

Not an interleaved A/B. Scene and voxel-map match the confirmation table above, so the second frame costs nothing while pipelines compile. Environment and model report `ready` later because it now covers the first frame that shows the scene. Model still settles about 300 ms after `ready`, with 78 to 121 ms of main-thread work. The census shows the hidden preview frame building the same ToyCar pipelines, which it starts once the deferred preview's hold ends; that is the likely cause, also of the model `cold` cell above.

### Pasting and scrolling a 100 KB script

A paste of 4 KB into `typed.ts` lays out 13,000 to 15,000 of the frame's roughly 15,000 layout objects in one forced layout of 35 to 41 ms, and shapes text for about 23 ms. The visible lines are about 600 of those objects; the rest is the `<textarea>`, which holds the whole file. It already has `wrap="off"` and `white-space: pre`, and containment cannot stop a value change from laying out its own text. Only a textarea that holds the visible lines would.

A wheel step lays out about 600 objects in 5 ms. The rest of its main-thread time is spread out: commit about 5 ms, paint 3.5 ms, script 3.5 ms. The viewport update already runs in the frame of the scroll event, so there is no frame of delay to remove.
